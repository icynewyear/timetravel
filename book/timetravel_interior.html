OPERATIONAL GUIDE
HOW TO NAVIGATE TIME TRAVEL

This is not a linear book of poetry. You do not turn from page to page in chronological sequence. Instead, Timetravel is an interactive labyrinth structured across one hundred distinct sections. At the conclusion of almost every passage, you will be presented with a fork in the road—a visceral decision reflecting bodily states, chemical interventions, hallucinated mythologies, motel corridors, and relational risks.

To begin, turn to Section 1 on page 1. Read the verse, feel the weight of its cadence, and examine the choices printed at the bottom of the section. When you decide which path to take, turn directly to the indicated Section number. Do not read the intervening pages. In physical gamebooks, turning the page is an intentional act of propulsion; you leap across the volume just as memory leaps across trauma and time.

Some paths will circle back on themselves, echoing the perseverative loops of psychiatric instability and insomnia. Other paths will terminate at one of four canonical endings—transcendent resting points where the storm recedes and survival is realized. If you encounter an [ENDING] marker, you have completed one timeline. Bookmark it, rest your eyes, and when you are ready, turn back to Section 1 to enter a different branch of the architecture.

Every single poem in this book is reachable. In the back of this volume, an Alphabetical Concordance is provided so that after you have wandered the labyrinth, you may locate any specific poem or archival plate directly.

I AM I
A POST-EQUILIBRIUM RETROSPECTIVE

Between 2014 and 2022, I lived inside the storm. These passages were not drafted as polished literary exercises for a salon; they were emergency telemetry. When you are moving through severe bipolar cycles, manic flights where angels whisper behind your skull, sensory overloads, medication titrations that leave you either shaking or numb, and long nights staring at motel ceiling tiles, writing is not a hobby—it is an anchor dropped into a fathomless ocean.

I originally built Timetravel inside Twine because linear form could not hold the multiplicity of those states. In a traditional book, a poem sits quietly on the page, pretending that grief and clarity follow a straight line. But madness is branched. Chemistry is branched. Every morning and every evening presents a fork: Do I take the pills? Do I smash the plate? Do I look at the bees? Do I get in the car and drive seventy miles per hour down highway seventy-six?

To hold this physical book today in 2026—bound in ink, paper, and hard-won somatic peace—is proof that equilibrium is possible. The rust goddess does not own me. The angels have laid down their swords. The dog snores beside the bed, the morning sun breaks over the Oregon ridge, and the timeline holds. I am I.
